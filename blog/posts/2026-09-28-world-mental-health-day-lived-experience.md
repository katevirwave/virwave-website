# Lived Experiences Heard: Why VirWave Has No Streaks

On 10 October, World Mental Health Day asks the world to listen. This year's theme, set by the World Federation for Mental Health, is **"Lived Experiences Heard: Real Voices, Real Change."**

It's a theme close to how VirWave began.

## Built from lived experience

VirWave started with our founder, Kate Julia, and a simple frustration: being told to "just calm down" has never helped anyone calm down. Her TEDx talk, *The Myth of Calm*, is about exactly that. Calm isn't a switch you flip or a personality you either have or don't. It's something you practise, in your own way, at your own pace.

Most wellbeing apps don't work like that. They count streaks, send guilt-tinged reminders, and quietly turn rest into another thing to fail at. People with lived experience of anxiety, burnout or a nervous system that runs hot told us the same thing again and again: *the pressure to be consistent was the part that made it worse.*

So we built VirWave differently:

- **No streaks, no guilt.** Miss a week? Nothing breaks. Come back when you need it.
- **Breathe your way.** Pick a shape, set a rhythm, and change it as you go. There's no single "right" way to breathe.
- **Your words, your pace.** Journaling and gentle connection with people you trust, without an audience or a score.

## What "being heard" looks like in a product

Listening to lived experience isn't a campaign; it changes the details. It's why sessions wait for you to press Begin instead of rushing you. It's why the app works with the text and counting turned off, for people who find them distracting. And it's why we write "helps you practise calm" and never "cures anxiety". VirWave is a tool for regulation, not a treatment.
